Proposed · AAAI 2027 Spring Symposium Series

Knowing When Not to Answer

Uncertainty, Abstention, and Disagreement in AI Systems

March 22–24, 2027  ·  Santa Clara University, Santa Clara, California, USA

This symposium has been proposed to the AAAI 2027 Spring Symposium Series. The proposal is under review; AAAI will announce accepted symposia on December 8, 2026. If accepted, the call for papers and submission dates will be posted here.

01About

AI systems are increasingly expected to say “I don’t know.” Yet most work on hallucination, abstention, uncertainty quantification, and LLM-as-a-judge evaluation reduces a system’s epistemic state to a single number. That number conflates situations that call for different actions:

  • Ignorance: the system lacks evidence. It should retrieve, ask, or defer.
  • Conflict: the evidence, sources, judges, or stakeholders disagree. It should surface the disagreement, not average it away.
  • Ill-posedness: the question has no determinate answer. It should reframe.

What should an AI system do when it does not know, when its evidence conflicts, or when the question has no single answer — and how do we measure whether it does so well?

The symposium brings together machine learning researchers working on LLM reliability with communities that have long had a vocabulary for these distinctions: belief functions, bilattices and Belnap–Dunn logic, paraconsistent and many-valued logics, subjective logic, and decision-making under non-probabilistic uncertainty.

02Tracks

T1. Abstention, hallucination, and knowing what you don’t know

Abstention and deferral in LLMs and agents; hallucination detection; selective and conformal prediction; epistemic vs. aleatoric uncertainty; evidential deep learning.

T2. Conflicting evidence in retrieval and multi-source reasoning

Knowledge conflicts in RAG; inter-document and context-memory contradictions; self-contradiction; evidence combination; source trust and provenance.

T3. Evaluating AI under disagreement

LLM-as-a-judge reliability; rater and model disagreement as signal; construct validity of benchmarks; calibration beyond a single score; synthetic respondents.

T4. Agents that defer, ask, and escalate

Human-in-the-loop handoff; clarifying questions; cost-sensitive abstention in tool-using agents; failure diagnosis in agentic pipelines.

T5. Formal foundations of indeterminacy

Belief functions, bilattices, paraconsistent and many-valued logics, bilateral evaluation, subjective logic, imprecise probabilities, and their use in ML pipelines.

T6. Disagreement in values, governance, and decisions

Value pluralism and alignment; AI governance and auditing under uncertainty; multi-criteria decision-making with conflicting or incomplete assessments; costs of abstention in health, law, education, and public administration.

03Format

Two and a half days designed for discussion: keynotes, paper sessions by track with extended discussion, a shared target problem where ignorance, conflict, and ill-posedness are labeled and participants compare what their methods can distinguish, working groups, and a poster session. Full papers (6–8 pages) and short/position papers (2–4 pages) in AAAI format; work in progress is welcome.

Following AAAI policy, all presentations are in person, and all participants must register.

06Background

The symposium builds on the AAAI 2026 Fall Symposium “Aligning with Whom? Pluriversal and Paraconsistent Frameworks for Value Alignment in AI Systems” and broadens its scope from alignment to uncertainty and disagreement in AI systems in general.
